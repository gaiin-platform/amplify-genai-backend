# Research summary

## User
Turn this into a short talk for a department seminar.

## Assistant
Our lab studied sleep and exam performance across 3 semesters with 860 undergraduates. Students averaging under 6 hours of sleep in exam week scored 11 points lower on average than those sleeping 7-8 hours. The effect held after controlling for GPA and course load. Caffeine use rose 40% in exam week but did not offset the sleep deficit. An intervention offering later exam start times (10am vs 8am) improved average scores by 4 points and reduced self-reported stress. Limitations: self-reported sleep, single institution. Next steps: wearable-based sleep tracking and a multi-campus replication in 2027.
